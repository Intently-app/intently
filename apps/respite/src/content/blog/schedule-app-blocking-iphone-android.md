A daily limit is good at one thing: telling you when you have had enough. It is not good at the hours when you simply should not be on the apps at all, like the last hour before bed or the middle of the working day. That is what a schedule is for. Here is how to set one up in Respite on iPhone or Android, what the free plan allows, and a few combinations that work.

## How do I schedule app blocking in Respite?

1. **Add the apps first.** A schedule blocks apps you already track, so open Home and add them (or pick them in Limits and pauses). If the list is empty, the schedule editor says so.
2. **Open Schedules.** Go to the **You** tab, then **Limits and pauses**, then **Schedules**.
3. **Add a schedule.** Choose **Add a schedule**. You can start from a template or build your own.
4. **Set the days and hours.** Pick the days of the week, then a start and an end time.
5. **Choose what to block.** Under **Blocks**, pick the apps this schedule closes during its hours.
6. **Save.** It switches on and runs by itself.

On iPhone, schedules use Apple's Screen Time, so the block holds even when Respite is not open. See [how to block apps on iPhone](/blog/how-to-block-apps-iphone/) for the wider picture of what iPhone can and cannot block.

## What are the ready-made schedules?

Three templates fill in sensible defaults, which you can change:

| Template | Days | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Bedtime | Every day | 22:00 to 07:00 |
| Work hours | Monday to Friday | 09:00 to 17:00 |
| Morning | Every day | 06:00 to 09:00 |

A window that crosses midnight, like Bedtime, ends the next morning. The shortest window is 15 minutes.

## How do schedules behave?

- **A pass or a snooze does not lift a schedule.** A schedule is the firm one. If you want a gentle pause, use a daily limit or the pause itself.
- **A running schedule is locked.** It cannot be edited until it ends, so you cannot talk yourself out of it at 10:30 pm.
- **Times follow your phone's clock.** On a daylight saving night a window is an hour longer or shorter.
- **Up to eight schedules can be on at once.** More can exist, but only eight run together.

| | Free | Plus |

| Schedules you can have | 1 | Unlimited |

If you have more than one schedule on the free plan, Respite tells you "Only your first schedule is running", and the others are saved, not deleted. They start running again if you upgrade. The full list of what Plus includes is in [Respite Plus: everything that is included](/blog/respite-plus-whats-included/).

## Which schedules are worth having?

The best schedule is one you will not resent. Three that tend to stick:

- **Bedtime, social and video apps.** The single most effective schedule for most people; see [how to stop scrolling at night](/blog/stop-scrolling-at-night/).
- **Work hours, the two or three apps that eat your afternoon.** A short list beats a long one.
- **Morning, one app only.** If you check your phone before getting up, try [this morning routine](/blog/stop-checking-phone-morning/) first and add a schedule if it is not enough.

## Does scheduled blocking work, or do people just get around it?

Hard blocks help for short, defined stretches and are easier to get around the more of your life they cover. A schedule on the apps you struggle with, plus a pause for everything else, is a better mix than blocking everything. We looked at the evidence in [do app blockers actually work?](/blog/do-app-blockers-work/) and compare approaches in [pauses vs blocking](/blog/mindful-tech-interventions-vs-blocking/).

## Troubleshooting

- **My schedule is not running.** Check that it has days and apps chosen and is switched on. On the free plan only the first schedule runs.
- **It ran at the wrong time.** Times follow the phone's clock and time zone; check both.
- **I cannot edit it.** It is running now. Edit it after it ends.
