A pause only takes a few seconds, and then the moment is gone. Did you walk away from Instagram at 11 pm, or keep scrolling? Was that the third time today? Pause history keeps those moments so you can look back at them.

Pause history is a new free screen in Respite's Insights, on [iPhone, Android and Chrome](/download/). It lists the pauses from the last 14 days on the device you're using, and what you chose at each one.

## What does Pause history show?

Each pause is one row:

- **The app or site** it was for, with its icon.
- **The time** it happened.
- **What you chose:** Walked away, Continued, Snoozed, Continued past the limit, or Closed.
- **Your reason**, if you gave one when you continued, for example "Continued · Out of habit".

Rows are grouped by day: Today, Yesterday, then the date. Each day starts with a short summary such as "6 pauses, 2 walk-aways", so you can scan a fortnight in a few seconds.

Walk-aways are marked in green. Everything else stays neutral; nothing is shown in red. A pause is there to give you a moment to choose, and continuing is a fine choice when you meant it. The history is there to show patterns, not to grade you.

## How do I open it?

1. Open **Insights** in Respite, on your phone or in the Chrome extension.
2. Under the card that counts your walk-aways, tap **Pause history**, "Every pause and what you chose".
3. To see one app, tap its chip at the top. **All apps** brings everything back.

The row shows on the Day, Week and Month views. Pause history is about the device in your hand, so it appears when Insights is showing all devices or this device.

## What can you learn from it?

A list of pauses sounds dry, but a few patterns jump out quickly:

- **The time of day you give in.** If most of your "Continued" rows sit after 10 pm, a [schedule for the evening](/blog/schedule-app-blocking-iphone-android/) will do more than a tighter daily limit.
- **The app you walk away from easily**, and the one you never do. Filter by each app and compare. The stubborn one might need a [different way of stepping in](/blog/mindful-tech-interventions-vs-blocking/), such as a lock at the limit instead of a gentle pause.
- **Your reasons.** If "Out of habit" keeps showing up, that's the reflex the pause is there to catch. If it's mostly "A task", the app may be one you genuinely need, and its limit could be looser.
- **Snoozes that stack up.** Several snoozes in a row usually means the limit is set lower than the day really needs.

None of this needs a spreadsheet. Ten days of rows tell you more than a week of good intentions. For why a short pause works at all, see [the science of friction](/blog/science-of-friction/).

## What about privacy?

Pause history adds no new tracking. It reads the answers Respite already stores on your device when a pause happens, and shows them back to you. Nothing new is sent to your account, and each device shows only its own pauses.

On iPhone, app names and icons are drawn by Apple's Screen Time, the same way as everywhere else in Respite. An app you've stopped tracking shows as "Removed app".

If you use Respite Plus and have [connected your own AI](/blog/connect-claude-chatgpt-cursor-to-respite-mcp/), it can read your pauses and the reasons you gave as part of its read access ([what a connected AI can see](/ai/)), and help you spot the patterns above in plain words.

## Is it free?

Yes. Pause history is part of free Respite on every platform, with the pause itself, app, website and category limits, one schedule and streaks. [Respite Plus](/blog/respite-plus-whats-included/) adds sync across your devices, unlimited schedules, longer focus sessions and the AI connection.
