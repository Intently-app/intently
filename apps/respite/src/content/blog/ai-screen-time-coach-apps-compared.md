If you have searched "AI screen time coach" you have probably met a handful of apps with similar names, similar screenshots and one shared idea: instead of just blocking Instagram, an AI asks you why you want it. It is a good idea, and also a new category, so the details (platforms, prices, privacy) matter more than the marketing. Here is how the main ones compare and where a different approach, bringing your own AI, fits.

We make Respite, which is not an AI coach, so we say plainly where the coaches are better. Facts are from store listings and checked in November 2026; the sources are at the end.

## How do AI screen time coaches work?

The pattern is the same everywhere:

1. You choose apps to guard (on iPhone, using Apple's Family Controls and Screen Time frameworks).
2. When you open one, the app shows a chat instead of the app.
3. An AI asks why you want it. You answer; it may encourage, push back, offer an alternative or grant a short session.
4. The app records the exchange, and the better ones show your history.

The appeal is real: a conversation is a more personal kind of friction than a countdown. The risks are also real: you are giving an AI service your reasons and often your usage, and an AI that can be argued with will, sometimes, be argued with.

## What do the coaches do well?

- **They act at the right moment.** Habit change is mostly about the seconds before the tap. An in-app coach is there; a separate chat window is not.
- **They make you articulate a reason.** Saying "I am bored" out loud (or typing it) is itself an intervention, and a log of reasons is useful data.
- **They feel personal.** For some people a conversation lands where a timer does not.
- **They can grant timed access.** "Fifteen minutes, then it locks again" is a gentler rule than all-or-nothing.

If those are what you want, a coach is the right tool and Respite is not a substitute: its pause is a short, fixed check-in, and its AI connection is for reviewing and planning, not for talking you out of an app in the moment.

## What should you check before choosing one?

**Platform.** The coaches we found are iPhone or iOS only. If you use Android or want your limits in the browser too, that rules most of them out.

**Privacy.** Their AI is the maker's, so the conversation and your usage go to their service and their AI provider. Read the App Store privacy label, not just the description. For example, FOMO's listing describes on-device handling while its privacy label lists data linked to identity as of November 2026. There may be a clear explanation, but it is the kind of thing worth reading before you tell an app why you scroll at 2 am.

**Price.** Listed prices run around $9 to $10 a month, so $60 to $80 a year. That is more than most blockers. Free tiers exist but cap the AI (a daily message limit, for example).

**Evidence.** We found no independent studies of these apps. The reasoning is plausible; the results are the makers' claims. Judge by your own numbers after a week.

**Can it be talked round?** An AI that grants access can be persuaded. Some people find that a feature (it makes you think), others that it is a loophole. Strict modes and locked schedules, where available, cover that.

## What is the alternative: bring your own AI?

Respite's approach is the opposite way round. Rather than shipping a coach, it exposes your data over MCP so the assistant you already use can read it. To our knowledge Respite is the first mobile screen time app with a built-in MCP server (November 2026). With Respite Plus, you connect [Claude](/blog/connect-claude-to-screen-time/), [ChatGPT](/blog/connect-chatgpt-to-phone-usage/) or Cursor, and ask things like "which limit do I break most?" or "when I get paused at a limit, do I walk away?". [The hub](/ai/) has the full list of what it can read.

What that buys you: no second AI to pay for, no second company holding the conversation (your data goes to the provider you already chose), and coverage of Android, iPhone and Chrome. What it costs you: the AI does not speak up when you open an app, it needs you to ask, and it only makes limits stricter, if you opt in. Anything looser needs your approval in the Respite app, and every change is logged with undo.
